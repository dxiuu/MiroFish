## Trump First-Term Cabinet Departure History

Donald Trump's first term (2017-2021) set records for Cabinet turnover. Michael Flynn departed after just 24 days. Rex Tillerson was fired via Twitter after 14 months. Jeff Sessions was forced to resign after 21 months. John Bolton departed after policy disagreements on Iran and North Korea.

Historical patterns show: average time-to-departure for forced exits is 14 months; median time for first major departure is 6 months; peak risk windows are the first 100 days, post-midterm (months 22-26), and post-major-policy-failure. Departure triggers ranked by frequency: (1) policy disagreement or public contradiction, (2) loyalty test failure, (3) personal scandal, (4) political embarrassment, (5) restructuring.

**Second-term update:** Trump ended 2025 with zero Cabinet departures — a contrast with his first term (2 in the first year). The Noem departure on March 5, 2026 came at approximately month 13.5 of the second term, consistent with the 14-month historical average. Brookings tracks "A Team" turnover at 29% as of January 2026.
